# Benchmark and practical policy baselines for action-free object transfer

## Which OCBench evidence changes our experimental design?

### Takeaway
Keep OCBench as the simulation and data-generation infrastructure, but test policy competence before attributing failure to the inverse dynamics model (IDM). The benchmark paper motivates a small matched action-chunking pilot; it does not establish that our image-only, 50-episode geometry-transfer experiment will work.

### Cited Findings
- **Verified metadata:** Seohong Park and Sergey Levine, *Behavioral Cloning Mystery*, 2026, preprint. The repository's citation block uses `@misc`; no publication venue should be invented. Read the primary 28-page paper, particularly Sections 4–5 and the benchmark appendices. [Official PDF](https://seohong.me/projects/ocbench/ocbench.pdf), [official citation](https://github.com/seohongpark/ocbench#citation).
- **Paper evidence (Sections 4.1–4.2, pp. 4–5):** trajectories are deliberately narrow, temporally correlated/non-Markovian and semantically diverse. Experiments mainly use state observations, although pixels are supported. Standard environments run at 50 Hz; `lite` operates at 10 Hz with faster demonstrations. CPU and MJWarp are not numerically identical. [Paper](https://seohong.me/projects/ocbench/ocbench.pdf).
- **Paper evidence (Sections 5.1, 5.2, 5.2.1; pp. 5–8):** validation action/flow loss need not track task success; chunked policies can outperform single-step policies, and adding history can reduce prediction loss while hurting execution. These are controlled empirical findings, not a guarantee for our pixel-based task. [Paper](https://seohong.me/projects/ocbench/ocbench.pdf).
- **Pinned source contract:** inspected commit `e2cd2f72110b66bd65afab1b855d81ebc73aeacc`, dated `2026-10-06T05:39:23Z` by the GitHub commit API. Its manipulation environment instantiates UR5e/Robotiq, returns only rendered cameras for pixel observations, and converts six normalized joint increments plus a gripper increment into low-level targets. `compute_ob_info` and `info` remain privileged and must not be fed to learned models. [Commit](https://github.com/seohongpark/ocbench/commit/e2cd2f72110b66bd65afab1b855d81ebc73aeacc), [manipulation_env.py: `compute_observation`, `get_pixel_observation`, `_set_joint_delta_control`](https://github.com/seohongpark/ocbench/blob/e2cd2f72110b66bd65afab1b855d81ebc73aeacc/ocbench/envs/manipulation_env.py).
- **Dense data is justified:** published visual collection commands specify `observation_interval=25`. The reference training configuration rejects sparse images on CPU, requires offline visual MJWarp, and requires chunk length equal to the image interval. Using dense `observation_interval=1` avoids that sparse-observation restriction and supplies the adjacent frames needed by our IDM. [Collection commands](https://github.com/seohongpark/ocbench/blob/e2cd2f72110b66bd65afab1b855d81ebc73aeacc/data_gen_scripts/commands.sh), [training_utils.py: `setup_agent_config`](https://github.com/seohongpark/ocbench/blob/e2cd2f72110b66bd65afab1b855d81ebc73aeacc/impls/utils/training_utils.py).
- **Episode alignment:** the documented format includes the final observation but no extra action; observation and action arrays can differ in length. Pair windows within episodes using that contract rather than treating array indices as globally interchangeable. [Dataset format](https://github.com/seohongpark/ocbench#datasets).
- **BC/FBC implementations:** both are JAX/Flax agents, accept observations/actions, and can construct a configured visual encoder. BC supports L1 or L2 regression; FBC uses flow matching and Euler sampling. Their existence is code evidence, not successful execution here. [bc.py](https://github.com/seohongpark/ocbench/blob/e2cd2f72110b66bd65afab1b855d81ebc73aeacc/impls/agents/bc.py), [fbc.py](https://github.com/seohongpark/ocbench/blob/e2cd2f72110b66bd65afab1b855d81ebc73aeacc/impls/agents/fbc.py).
- **Multi-camera support has a specific path:** `ConvNeXtEncoder` explicitly handles `(..., cameras, H, W, C)`, shares image weights, and concatenates camera features. IMPALA's existence alone does not prove correct reduction of a separate camera axis. Frame stacking concatenates channels. Critically, `history_fbc` explicitly rejects visual environments; it is not an immediately usable image-history baseline. [encoders.py](https://github.com/seohongpark/ocbench/blob/e2cd2f72110b66bd65afab1b855d81ebc73aeacc/impls/utils/encoders.py), [FrameStackWrapper](https://github.com/seohongpark/ocbench/blob/e2cd2f72110b66bd65afab1b855d81ebc73aeacc/impls/envs/env_utils.py), [history guard](https://github.com/seohongpark/ocbench/blob/e2cd2f72110b66bd65afab1b855d81ebc73aeacc/impls/utils/training_utils.py).
- **Published commands are not our budget:** supplied state-based `lite` BC/FBC commands use 2 million updates, batch size 1024, eight 1024-wide hidden layers and action chunks of three. No visual command appeared in the inspected reference command file. [Training commands](https://github.com/seohongpark/ocbench/blob/e2cd2f72110b66bd65afab1b855d81ebc73aeacc/impls/commands.sh).

### Inferences
- **Recommended pilot, not an unapproved proposal change:** compare the current image-only temporal BC with the same backbone predicting a three-action chunk (0.3 seconds at 10 Hz). Hold image history, cameras, training data and capacity approximately fixed; compare source success and supervised-reference target success before choosing the backbone for all IDM ablations. Report prediction horizon and executed horizon separately. Three is an initialization from the reference `lite` command, not a verified optimum. [Motivation](https://seohong.me/projects/ocbench/ocbench.pdf), [reference configuration](https://github.com/seohongpark/ocbench/blob/e2cd2f72110b66bd65afab1b855d81ebc73aeacc/impls/commands.sh).
- Once selected, use that same policy and chunking schedule for source-only, unadapted IDM, adapted IDM, policy alignment, and supervised reference. This isolates the label/adaptation effect instead of confounding it with a stronger controller. Do not interpret 50 source demonstrations as literature-validated sufficiency. [Design motivation](https://seohong.me/projects/ocbench/ocbench.pdf).
- Training loss alone is a weak policy-selection proxy. Prefer source rollout success for policy feasibility/selection, with source validation error for IDM diagnostics; disclose any target-development rollout feedback. This is our methodological response to the paper, not a theorem. [Evidence](https://seohong.me/projects/ocbench/ocbench.pdf).

### Gaps
- No simulator, training job, image loader or policy rollout was run in this review. Geometry changes, target expert grasps, GPU cost and the 50-episode budget remain unvalidated.
- The PDF is an unversioned author-hosted preprint; its current content was inspected, but a stable release date/version was not established. Pin the code and record a PDF checksum when archiving experiment provenance.
- Upstream moved during inspection: live HEAD was `6939a1e84f7ce9dbdd20c9f80d4cf335e8f83b49`, with timestamp `2026-10-07T01:56:47Z` (Oct 6 in Toronto). All consequential code links above deliberately use the existing project pin, avoiding silent reliance on moving HEAD.

## Which policy baseline should we actually run and cite?

### Takeaway
The core scientific baseline is unadapted-IDM pseudo-labeling with the same policy as the proposed method. For an additional published policy, retain **image-only Diffusion Policy as the preferred PyTorch candidate**, subject to a cheap integration/compute pilot; use OCBench FBC as the alternative if adopting its JAX training stack. Neither is itself an action-free learning method.

### Cited Findings
- **Diffusion Policy:** Cheng Chi, Siyuan Feng, Yilun Du, Zhenjia Xu, Eric Cousineau, Benjamin Burchfiel and Shuran Song, *Diffusion Policy: Visuomotor Policy Learning via Action Diffusion*, RSS 2023. It predicts action sequences through conditional denoising and executes them with receding-horizon control. It addresses policy modeling, not recovering unknown actions from videos. Read Sections III–IV. The original RSS citation is distinct from the later IJRR version and its different author list. [RSS paper](https://diffusion-policy.cs.columbia.edu/diffusion_policy_2023.pdf), [official citation](https://diffusion-policy.cs.columbia.edu/).
- **DP's generic implementation can accept RGB-only input:** `DiffusionUnetImagePolicy` takes an observation encoder and observation-shape metadata; `MultiImageObsEncoder` processes configurable RGB keys and separately iterates over low-dimensional keys, which may be empty. Thus image-only configuration is supported structurally; a correct OCBench dataset/runner/normalizer still needs implementation and a runtime check. Do not describe this as reproducing the original benchmark protocol unchanged. [Policy code](https://github.com/real-stanford/diffusion_policy/blob/5ba07ac6661db573af695b419a7947ecb704690f/diffusion_policy/policy/diffusion_unet_image_policy.py), [multi-image encoder](https://github.com/real-stanford/diffusion_policy/blob/5ba07ac6661db573af695b419a7947ecb704690f/diffusion_policy/model/vision/multi_image_obs_encoder.py).
- **ACT:** Tony Z. Zhao, Vikash Kumar, Sergey Levine and Chelsea Finn, *Learning Fine-Grained Bimanual Manipulation with Low-Cost Hardware*, RSS 2023. Its CVAE policy learns action chunks with temporal ensembling. Original observations include multi-view RGB **and joint positions**, and commands are target joints. Figure 4 and Section V make this explicit. Removing proprioception is an architectural/protocol adaptation, not standard ACT. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.13705), [reference training code](https://github.com/tonyzhaozh/act/blob/742c753c0d4a5d87076c8f69e5628c79a8cc5488/imitate_episodes.py).

### Inferences
- **Ranked implementation choice:** (1) same-backbone BC with/without IDM adaptation is mandatory; (2) a small chunked-BC pilot is a practical control; (3) one image-only Diffusion Policy comparison is preferred if its integration and training fit; (4) repository FBC is an alternative, not another mandatory architecture; (5) ACT is background only unless the team explicitly chooses to implement its image-only adaptation. [DP code](https://github.com/real-stanford/diffusion_policy/blob/5ba07ac6661db573af695b419a7947ecb704690f/diffusion_policy/model/vision/multi_image_obs_encoder.py), [FBC code](https://github.com/seohongpark/ocbench/blob/e2cd2f72110b66bd65afab1b855d81ebc73aeacc/impls/agents/fbc.py), [ACT paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.13705).
- Run DP first on exactly A plus the same unadapted pseudo-labeled B videos. This asks whether policy class alone changes performance; it does not isolate the adversarial contribution. If resources allow, run DP with adapted pseudoactions too, turning it into a transfer-of-benefit check. Match observations, pseudo-labels, resets and tuning access, and explicitly report remaining backbone/compute differences. [Policy-method basis](https://diffusion-policy.cs.columbia.edu/diffusion_policy_2023.pdf).
- **Core citation ranking within this subtopic:** OCBench first (infrastructure and experimental pitfalls); Diffusion Policy second (if retained as implemented comparison). ACT is optional background for chunking; it is not required merely because ALOHA appeared in early discussions. [OCBench](https://seohong.me/projects/ocbench/ocbench.pdf), [DP](https://diffusion-policy.cs.columbia.edu/), [ACT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13705).

### Gaps
- No fair empirical comparison between FBC and DP exists for our exact data/input/object-shift protocol; the preference for DP is an engineering inference from the current PyTorch plan and configurable input code, not evidence of superiority.
- The course may require comparisons with modern methods; a published architecture comparison does not replace related work and a meaningful baseline in action-free learning itself.

## Do latent-action and large video-policy papers belong in the core set?

### Takeaway
Use **LAPA** to explain the distinction between action-free pretraining and action-supervised downstream adaptation; use **MotoVLA** to avoid an incorrect novelty claim about target skills seen only in unlabeled video. UniVLA and DreamZero are optional positioning, not semester implementation commitments.

### Cited Findings
- **LAPA:** *Latent Action Pretraining from Videos*, Seonghyeon Ye et al., ICLR 2025 (initial arXiv 2024; inspected v2, May 15, 2025). Section 3 learns discrete latent actions from image pairs, predicts them from observations/instructions, then fine-tunes on robot action data. Section 4.1/Table 3 includes 100 labeled SIMPLER fine-tuning trajectories and 450 real-world trajectories. Thus “no action labels during pretraining” does not mean “no labels for downstream tasks.” Section 4.6 reports 272 H100-hours for the best Open-X pretraining run; it is not a from-scratch course baseline. The underlying VLM is 7B. Appendix C also gives a useful small precedent: ResNet-18 plus MLP for its VPT-style two-frame IDM. [Full paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.11758v2), [metadata](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.11758).
- **MotoVLA:** Alexander Spiridonov, Jan-Nico Zaech, Nikolay Nikolov, Luc Van Gool and Danda Pani Paudel, *Generalist Robot Manipulation beyond Action Labeled Data*, CoRL 2025 according to the authors' arXiv record. Section 4.4 explicitly distinguishes tasks unseen everywhere from tasks seen in action-free pretraining but absent from action training. That is materially relevant to our framing. Sections 3.1–3.4 use dynamic 3D point-cloud training and later action alignment, with proprioceptive observations in the labeled stage. It is not our strict RGB-only deployment contract. [Paper, especially Section 4.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19958v1), [metadata](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19958).
- **UniVLA:** the inspected May 2025 version uses a 7B VLM with latent actions; Section IV-A's LIBERO experiments explicitly use supervised fine-tuning in the target task suite. Section III-C uses ground-truth low-level action loss; Appendix A reports approximately 960 A100-hours for the main policy pretraining. It is relevant for task-centric latent representations, not evidence that the same protocol solves our unlabeled B problem. [Full paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.06111v1).
- **DreamZero:** *World Action Models are Zero-shot Policies*, Seonghyeon Ye et al., February 2026 preprint. Its 14B video/action model and large robot-data training make it context, not an appropriate from-scratch course baseline. Section 5 includes video-only cross-embodiment transfer. The paper distinguishes task progress and full success; reporting progress as success would be inaccurate. [Full paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.15922v1), [metadata](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15922).

### Inferences
- **Reading priority:** LAPA Sections 3–4/Table 3 and MotoVLA Section 4.4 are the useful additions beyond the direct IDM/DA papers covered elsewhere. Cite LAPA in a short alternatives paragraph and MotoVLA when qualifying novelty. Read UniVLA/DreamZero only if expanding the final report's broader landscape. [LAPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.11758v2), [MotoVLA](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19958v1).
- A reduced latent-action model trained on A/B videos and mapped using A labels would be an interesting separate design, but the papers above do not establish that this reduced protocol works under our object shift. Do not add it to the mandatory experimental matrix. [LAPA's actual protocol](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.11758v2).

### Gaps
- Full reproduction feasibility, checkpoints, current package compatibility and hardware requirements of the large latent/video models were not tested. No claim of current best performance or global literature completeness is made.
- MotoVLA's exact total training compute was not established. Its relevance here is the action-label split and alternative representation, not a compute comparison.
