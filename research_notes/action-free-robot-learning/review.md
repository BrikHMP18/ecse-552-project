# Selecting papers that sharpen action-free transfer

*Literature review for ECSE 552 · 7 October 2026 · Recommendations for team review; no experiments performed.*

**The strongest reading set is BCO, Giving Robots a Hand, AMPLIFY, DANN, Zhao et al. on invariant representations, OCBench, and Diffusion Policy.** Together, they explain the learning mechanism, establish close robotic precedents, expose the assumptions behind adversarial alignment, and guide a practical experiment. The proposed study uses labeled cube demonstrations and action-free videos of an elongated block in OCBench; both learned models receive images rather than simulator states. Its defensible contribution is a controlled investigation of whether adapting an inverse dynamics model improves transfer across object geometry, and whether alignment belongs in that model or the policy. Learning control from videos and transferring to tasks absent from action training already have precedents. The recommendations below therefore prioritize a clear supervision contract, matched comparisons, and informative failure analysis over a broad architecture survey. They refine the current proposal without treating implementation choices or feasibility as settled.

## Three predecessors define the mechanism and its assumptions

**Behavioral Cloning from Observation (BCO)** provides the essential methodological starting point. Torabi, Warnell and Stone train an agent-specific inverse dynamics model from transitions with known actions, infer actions for observation-only demonstrations, then train a policy. Their training uses agent exploration; BCO(0) avoids further interaction after observing demonstrations, whereas iterative variants collect additional interaction. This distinguishes the original procedure from our fixed, offline source dataset. Read Sections 3–4 for the mechanism and interaction assumptions, rather than treating BCO as an unchanged image-based object-transfer baseline. ([BCO, IJCAI 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.01954))

For this project, my recommendation is to call the indispensable comparison **unadapted-IDM pseudo-labeling**, explaining that it follows the BCO pattern. It should use exactly the same source episodes, target videos, policy architecture and optimization access as the proposed method. Its purpose is to answer whether target video helps before adding adversarial learning. Reimplementing every component of original BCO would introduce a different interaction protocol and obscure that question. Source-only cloning remains necessary because improvements over an ineffective pseudo-labeling baseline alone would not show that target videos provide useful supervision.

**Giving Robots a Hand** is a particularly useful robotic precedent. Kim, Wu and Finn train an IDM using diverse, action-labeled robot play, label human demonstrations recorded from a masked eye-in-hand view, and combine inferred and real actions for imitation. Two details matter: play coverage is broader than narrow task demonstrations, and the policy also receives a binary grasp-state variable. During human-video training, that variable is estimated from the preceding inferred grasp action. Thus its policy is not the exact images-only contract proposed here, despite image-based inverse prediction. Read Sections 4.1–4.3 and Appendix A.3. Cite the verified 2023 preprint rather than assigning an unverified conference venue. ([Giving Robots a Hand](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.05959))

The design implication is substantial: **50 cube demonstrations are a hypothesis about sufficient action coverage, not a validated recipe**. An elongated block can require approach angles, wrist motions or gripper timing missing from those trajectories. Adversarial alignment cannot be assumed to recover commands for unrepresented motions. I recommend measuring source IDM errors by action component and manipulation phase, and reviewing which motions actually occur in the demonstrations. If a separate source-play collection becomes necessary, count its action labels in the budget and provide the same collection to every applicable method. This changes the data allocation transparently without quietly giving the proposed method privileged experience.

**AMPLIFY** is the closest modern reference in this selection for target tasks seen in videos but absent from action training. Collins and colleagues learn quantized motion from point tracks, train a forward motion predictor with videos, and decode predicted motion into executable actions with an IDM. Its task-generalization experiment trains the forward model on all LIBERO subsets while restricting action training to LIBERO-90. However, the robot-data contract includes proprioception, and the IDM conditions on images, proprioception and motion tokens; inference uses predicted motion rather than simply labeling all target videos offline and training BC. Read Section 2 and Section 3.2/Table 5. Official code is available, but adapting this multistage system is a separate implementation commitment. ([AMPLIFY paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.14198), [official code](https://github.com/pairlab/AMPLIFY))

My interpretation is that AMPLIFY should shape the project's positioning even if it is not reproduced. The relevant distinction is not that previous work cannot use target videos without target actions. It is that this course experiment isolates a simpler image-only IDM and tests where domain alignment helps under a controlled geometry change. A useful final report can compare the assumptions and mechanisms closely without presenting a reduced reproduction as the published AMPLIFY method. The team should decide separately whether a lightweight motion representation is an extension worth testing after the main comparisons work.

## Alignment is a testable intervention, not a guarantee

**DANN** supplies the adversarial training mechanism: a shared representation supports source-label prediction while a domain discriminator learns to distinguish source and target; gradient reversal opposes the domain objective at the encoder. Unlabeled target examples contribute to alignment. Applying that mechanism to a continuous-action robotic IDM is our design adaptation, not a ready-made robotic algorithm established by the original paper. Read its architecture, objective and optimization sections. **ADDA** is a distinct alternative with separate source and target encoders and staged adaptation; implementing both is unnecessary for the initial question. ([DANN, JMLR 2016](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v17/15-239.html), [ADDA, CVPR 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.05464))

**On Learning Invariant Representations for Domain Adaptation** is the key cautionary reading. Zhao and colleagues demonstrate that invariant representations with small source error are insufficient under conditional shift, and analyze conflicts involving label distributions. This is classification theory, not a theorem proving failure of a continuous-control IDM. Its relevance is an explicit analogy: when domain membership correlates with different motions or grasps, suppressing domain information can also suppress useful action information. Read the counterexample and assumptions surrounding the bounds, not merely the abstract. ([Zhao et al., ICML 2019](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/zhao19a.html))

Consequently, I would preserve the proposal's question about alignment location but replace any implied ordering of methods with an open hypothesis. IDM-only alignment might remove object appearance that distracts from motion estimation; it might instead erase geometric information needed to infer contact or grasp commands. Policy alignment faces a related tension because the new object's geometry can legitimately change the required behavior. Chance-level domain discrimination is therefore a diagnostic, never the success criterion. The comparison needs action-component error after model selection and robot execution on matched resets, including source retention.

**GDA is relevant but cannot serve unchanged as a zero-target-action comparator.** Cheng and colleagues study sim-and-real policy co-training with action-labeled target demonstrations. Their action-aware optimal-transport formulation uses a joint cost; the implemented cost substitutes proprioceptive states for actions, and trajectory sampling uses proprioceptive alignment. The policy also receives proprioception. The paper considers more than superficial appearance changes, including geometry-related generalization, so dismissing it as merely a texture method would be inaccurate. Cite it as supervised adaptation context, while making its different information access explicit. ([GDA, NeurIPS 2025](https://ot-sim2real.github.io/), [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.18631))

A practical consequence concerns model selection. Source validation can detect damage to source prediction, but it cannot establish the best target alignment strength. I recommend a small declared set of alignment weights, a source-based selection rule fixed before target evaluation, and a sensitivity analysis reported after selection. If target-development success influences task or hyperparameter choices, disclose that feedback precisely. Hidden target actions must not choose weights, checkpoints or pseudo-label thresholds. The supervised target reference remains a separate experiment with additional labels, not a mathematical upper bound.

## OCBench makes the study concrete but adds policy confounders

**Behavioral Cloning Mystery**, by Park and Levine, introduces OCBench and deliberately generates temporally correlated, diverse scripted demonstrations. Its experiments show that action loss and execution success can disagree, action chunking can matter strongly, and history can lower prediction error while hurting control. Most reported analysis uses state observations, so these findings motivate checks rather than predict our image-only outcome. The `lite` configuration offers shorter, lower-frequency tasks for iteration. Read Sections 4–5, especially the treatment of history and chunking. Cite the work as the verified 2026 preprint. ([OCBench paper](https://seohong.me/projects/ocbench/ocbench.pdf))

Source inspection at the project's pinned commit confirms practical details: the environment supplies UR5e/Robotiq manipulation, camera observations and seven normalized joint/gripper increments. Published visual datasets use sparse images, whereas our IDM requires control-step alignment. The training utilities impose additional sparse-data restrictions; generating dense images with `observation_interval=1` is therefore a justified project choice. BC and flow-BC reference agents support visual encoders in JAX/Flax, but the separate history-FBC agent explicitly rejects visual environments. These observations verify code paths, not runtime feasibility. ([Environment](https://github.com/seohongpark/ocbench/blob/e2cd2f72110b66bd65afab1b855d81ebc73aeacc/ocbench/envs/manipulation_env.py), [data commands](https://github.com/seohongpark/ocbench/blob/e2cd2f72110b66bd65afab1b855d81ebc73aeacc/data_gen_scripts/commands.sh), [training restrictions](https://github.com/seohongpark/ocbench/blob/e2cd2f72110b66bd65afab1b855d81ebc73aeacc/impls/utils/training_utils.py))

I recommend a small policy pilot before interpreting adaptation. Compare the proposed temporal BC with the same visual backbone predicting a short action chunk, using source demonstrations and source validation/rollouts only to select the shared configuration. Keep camera/history access matched and report both predicted and executed horizons. A three-step chunk is a reasonable starting configuration to investigate, not an established optimum. Freeze this choice for all main ablations. The separately labeled supervised target reference serves only as a task-feasibility gate; its results must not select the action-free methods' architecture or hyperparameters. If source control fails, fix the underlying learning task; if supervised target control fails, revisit the object or expert rather than declaring action-free transfer impossible.

**Diffusion Policy** is the preferred additional published-policy candidate if the project keeps PyTorch. Chi and colleagues model conditional action sequences through diffusion and use receding-horizon execution. This tests policy expressiveness rather than supplying missing action labels. Its generic multi-image encoder can be configured without low-dimensional inputs, although an OCBench dataset adapter, evaluation runner and normalization checks are still required. Standard **ACT** instead includes joint positions along with images; removing those inputs changes its original contract. DP is therefore the more straightforward image-only candidate from the inspected code, not a proven stronger method for this experiment. ([Diffusion Policy, RSS 2023](https://diffusion-policy.cs.columbia.edu/diffusion_policy_2023.pdf), [image encoder](https://github.com/real-stanford/diffusion_policy/blob/5ba07ac6661db573af695b419a7947ecb704690f/diffusion_policy/model/vision/multi_image_obs_encoder.py), [ACT, RSS 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13705))

The recommended minimum matrix remains source-only, unadapted IDM, IDM-only alignment, policy-only alignment and supervised reference with the same policy. An additional DP run should initially receive the identical unadapted pseudo-labels; otherwise a change in architecture and labels would be inseparable. If resources permit, repeating DP with adapted labels tests whether the adaptation benefit survives a stronger policy. OCBench flow BC is a reasonable alternative if the team chooses its JAX stack, but running both generative architectures would expand scope without necessarily improving the central causal comparison.

## Supporting papers clarify what action-free learning does and does not mean

**VPT** supports the offline-labeling design: Baker and colleagues train a noncausal IDM with labeled Minecraft trajectories, use it to label videos, and train a causal policy. The important transferable lesson is that future frames are legitimate for offline action inference but unavailable to the executing policy. Its scale and game interface are not a robotics replication target. Read Section 3 and the IDM/policy appendices. ([VPT, NeurIPS 2022](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/hash/9c7008aff45b5d8f0973b23e1a22ada0-Abstract-Conference.html))

**LAPA** distinguishes action-free pretraining from label-free downstream adaptation. Ye and colleagues learn discrete latent actions from video and pretrain a policy on those tokens, then fine-tune using robot action supervision; downstream experiments include labeled target-task data. **MotoVLA** is more directly relevant to the generalization vocabulary: it separately evaluates tasks absent from action training but present in action-free pretraining, using dynamic point-cloud representations and later action alignment. Its labeled stage includes proprioception. Neither should be described as an unchanged strict-RGB baseline for this project. ([LAPA, ICLR 2025, Sections 3–4](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.11758v2), [MotoVLA, Section 4.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19958v1))

**VERA** offers a useful optional direction for structured inverse prediction. It uses an image-conditioned Jacobian field, visual motion and action-supervised robot experience to translate video plans into control. That is a specific alternative to unconstrained action regression, not evidence that adversarial alignment will generalize across our objects. Its large video planner need not become a course dependency. **UniVLA** and **DreamZero** belong in broader positioning only: the former includes supervised downstream adaptation, while the latter uses a large video/action model; task progress and full success are distinct metrics in its evaluations. ([VERA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27817), [UniVLA](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.06111v1), [DreamZero](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.15922v1))

For proposal space, I recommend the seven core readings named at the start, with VPT and LAPA added only where their distinctions directly support a sentence. Give Giving Robots a Hand and AMPLIFY priority over a long list of foundation models because they materially change how the project should be designed and described. Keep ADDA, ACT, GDA, MotoVLA and VERA available for focused supporting discussion. Omit reproductions of UniVLA, DreamZero and interaction-based third-person imitation from the mandatory plan. Additional recent titles screened only at abstract level should not be presented as fully reviewed or used to claim exhaustive novelty.

## What the team can decide from this review

The immediate proposal edits to consider are concrete: acknowledge the two close robotic predecessors; distinguish play coverage from task demonstrations; describe DANN as a hypothesis under geometry shift; and add a policy-competence gate before adaptation. Clarify that the target domain is seen in video, that the supervised reference has extra information, and that a published policy comparison tests a different axis from the action-free baseline. These are recommendations for discussion, not changes already made to the manuscript.

The strongest outcome would explain a boundary: whether the available source motions support useful target action inference, and whether alignment preserves or destroys that capability. A well-controlled negative result can answer that question; an attractive aggregate success number with mixed information access cannot. No experiments, runtime validation or compute estimates have been established by this review. Primary papers and official source code ground the selection, while implementation feasibility and the final experimental budget remain pilot decisions.
